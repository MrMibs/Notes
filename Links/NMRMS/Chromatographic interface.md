Chromatography allows us to separate complex mixtures of **analytes as a function of time**. This **creates a lot of data** and makes interpretation hard. However, chromatography is really useful as it **reduces complexity** of the sample at any time-point (not in total however) as well as **improves sensitivity** as it is easier to see separations.

An example is with blood plasma which has a $10^9$ difference in concentration, which means albumin will drown the cytokine sample if you don't separate it over time. Chromatography works by putting your sample through a stationary phase, which seperates compounds based on interactions with the column.

A chromatographic column is based on eddie diffusion or convection, which tells you about a molecules path through a system. Further, it tells you about dispersion (if it stays on green or moves into hole) as well as resistance to mass transfer i.e. getting stuck in the column as shown on the right, interactions with the column.
![[Pasted image 20260528130802.png]]
This is described with the Van Deemter equation
$$H=A+B/u+C\cdot u$$
where
- H is the height equivalent to a theoretical plate i.e. column length need for one equilibrium step. Get this as low as possible.
- A is eddy diffusion i.e. the factor of having many holes and particles not going directly in which messes with separation.
- B is the longitudinal diffusion due to concentrations wanting to be equal. 
- C is the sample-column interaction coefficient (mass transfer coefficient) (just a bunch of constants in one)
- u is velocity of mobile system (we want this high but not too high due to Van Deemter minimum)
