#nmrms
We basically have a sample deposited in a solid support. You then bombard the solid with a gas, generating ions. MALDI instead requires you to put a liquid sample inside a matrix, then let it dry and put the crystal inside the MS. This means we cannot seperate analytes!
![[Pasted image 20260528145021.png]]

Requirements
- Soluble in wavelength
- Matrix needs to absorb wavelength at 337nm as that is our laser
- Low mass
- Inert

Common matricies are CHCA and DHB
![[Pasted image 20260528145132.png]]

If we mix the two matrices we get a good signal. This is e.g. ribonuclease B. Common matrices with ones we commonly use in our lab.
![[Pasted image 20260528145227.png]]

We are not entirely sure how MALDI works, what we know is that we shine a laser that disrupts the surface, then we explode the surface in about 550ns which generates ions. We think UV laser excites the matrix, transferring charges and explode the local environment which creates a plasma. They can then be accelerated using a magnetic field. Illustrated below.
![[Pasted image 20260528145353.png]]

Rapid fire facts:
This is a soft ionization technique (because we dont directly shoot with electrons and matrix eats energy)
The matrix is a solid crystalline (why do i care??)
Will primarily contain single charged species as well as M+H and M+Na ions
We ionize with laser
We dont really know the mechanism
Our absolute favorite method for initial testing, quick, works for everything, tolerates it all (except Liquid chromatography). Low mass region does not look very clean due to matrix ions.
