A Born–Haber cycle is just a way of breaking **formation of an ionic solid** into measurable energy steps and using Hess’ law.

---

## 1. Write the target reaction

Start with formation from elements in their standard states:

Example:  
$$
\text{Na(s)} + \tfrac{1}{2}\text{Cl}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{NaCl(s)}  
$$

---

## 2. Convert all elements to gaseous atoms (atomization step)

- Metal:  
$$  
\text{Na(s)} \rightarrow \text{Na(g)}  
$$

- Nonmetal:  
$$
  \tfrac{1}{2}\text{Cl}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{Cl(g)}  
  $$

This uses:
- atomization enthalpy (metal)
- bond dissociation enthalpy (nonmetal molecules)

---

## 3. Ionize the metal atom
$$
\text{Na(g)} \rightarrow \text{Na}^+(g) + e^-  
$$

(Add higher ionization energies only if the problem requires it, e.g. Na²⁺ in hypothetical cases)

---

## 4. Add electrons to the nonmetal atom (electron affinity)

$$
\text{Cl(g)} + e^- \rightarrow \text{Cl}^-(g)  
$$

If multiple atoms are present, multiply accordingly.

---

## 5. Form the ionic lattice (lattice energy step)

$$
\text{Na}^+(g) + \text{Cl}^-(g) \rightarrow \text{NaCl(s)}  
$$

This step releases lattice energy.

---

## 6. Apply Hess’ Law

Sum all steps to match the overall reaction:

$$
\Delta H_f^\circ =  
\text{atomization terms}

- \text{ionization energy}
    
- \text{electron affinity}
    
- \text{lattice energy}  
$$

---

## 7. Check signs carefully

- Atomization: positive (energy in)
- Ionization: positive (energy in)
- Electron affinity: negative (energy out)
- Lattice energy: negative (energy out, formation)

---

## Quick checklist for exams

1. Write formation equation
2. Break into gas atoms
3. Ionize metal
4. Add electrons to nonmetal
5. Form lattice
6. Sum all enthalpies
7. Check sign and units

Generally
$\Delta H_{f}^{\circ}=x \Delta H_{\mathrm{atm}}(M)+\frac{y}{2} D\left(X_{2}\right)+x \mathrm{IE}+y \mathrm{EA}+U$

formation=atomise solid metal+break nonmetal bonds+ionise metal+add electrons to nonmetal+lattice formation
