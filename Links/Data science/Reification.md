#datascience
## Definition
Treating an abstract concept, model, or statistical construct as if it were a real physical thing.

## Why it is a problem
Because every model involves choices:

- What variables to include
- What variables to ignore
- How to measure things
- What assumptions to make

If you reify the model, you stop questioning those choices.

**All models are simplifications.**  
**Reification is forgetting the simplification part.**

## Example
Suppose we define a variable called "customer loyalty" using:

- Number of purchases
- Membership duration
- App usage

and combine them into a loyalty score.

There is no physical thing called "customer loyalty" that we directly measured. We created a statistical construct that hopefully captures it.

Reification happens when someone says:

> "This customer has a loyalty score of 82, therefore they possess 82 units of loyalty."

The score is merely one way of measuring an abstract idea.

## Warning signs
- Speaking of model outputs as if they are directly observable entities.
- Forgetting that measurements are approximations.

## How to avoid it
- Remember that variables and constructs are operational definitions.
- Distinguish between the concept and its measurement.

## Key idea
The map is not the territory.
