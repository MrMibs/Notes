**Nanoscale zero-valent iron (nZVI)** is extremely small particles of **metallic iron, Fe⁰**, typically **1–100 nm** in size, used mainly for **environmental remediation** because they are very reactive reducing agents. Over the past years, nanoscale zero-valent Iron has been progressively applied to water and soil remediation, due to its adsorption and redox properties

### The Chemical synthesis
NaBH4, sodium borohydride, is here used to \[reduce] iron from the oxidation state \[+3] to the oxidation state \[0]. NaBH4 is \[an ionic] compound, which can be written as \[Na+ \[BH4]‑]. The bond between boron and hydrogen has mainly \[covalent] character. \[H] is the atom that is formally \[oxidized] from oxidation state \[‑1] to oxidation state \[+1].

Nitrogen gas is added.

Severe plastic deformation → Physical method,

Electrolysis-based method → Chemical method,

Biomineralization → Green method,

Reduction with sodium borohydride → Chemical method,

Vapor deposition → Physical method,

Carbothermal reduction → Chemical method,

Precision milling → Physical method


2Fe2+(aq) + BH4−(aq) + 3H2O ---> 2Fe0 + H3BO3(aq) + 3H+(aq) → Aqueous phase reduction,

Fe(C2H3O2)2 + C ---> Fe0 + 2CH2CO + CO + H2O → Carbothermal reduction
