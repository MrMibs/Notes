
The most important material in our civilization, because:
1. Abundant and easy to mine.
2. The common ore can be easily and cheaply processed to obtain the metal.
3. The metal is malleable and ductile.
4. The melting point (1535°C) is low enough that the liquid phase can be handled without great difficulty.
5. By the addition of small quantities of other elements, alloys that have exactly the required combinations of strength, hardness, or ductility for very specific uses can be formed.

Chemical reactivity: easy oxidation of iron is a major disadvantage


## Blast furnace
#inorganic 
25 - 60 m in height, up to 14 m in diameter.

Constructed of steel with a lining of a heat and corrosion-resistant ceramic bricks. The main ceramic material used for the lining is aluminum oxide (corundum), although the lining of the lower parts of the furnace consists of ceramic oxides of formula AlxCr(2–x)O3, which has an improved resistance to the corrosion.

Hematite Fe2O3, Magnetite Fe3O4, and limonite FeO(OH)·nH2O

At temperatures between 1200 °C and 1500 °C, the iron melts and sinks to the bottom of the furnace.

Two tap-holes that are plugged with clay:
-the lower one for the denser iron metal the upper one for the less dense slag.
-Blast furnaces are run 24/7 and produce from 1000 to 10 000 tons of iron / day

The molten metal is usually conveyed directly in the liquid form to steelmaking plants.

On top: CaCO3(s) $\rightarrow$ CaO (s) + CO2(g)

Dawn: Acid-base reaction between the basic metallic oxide, CaO, and the acidic nonmetal oxide, SiO2:

CaO(s)+ SiO2(s) $\rightarrow$ CaSiO3 (l)

Other minor components are also reduced
MnO + C $\rightarrow$ Mn + CO
P2O5 + 5C $\rightarrow$ 2P+ 5CO

The slag can be either cooled to the solid phase, ground, and used in concrete manufacture or, while liquid, mixed with air and cooled into a “woolly” material that can be used for thermal insulation.

“Pig Iron” (iron produced by the blast furnace) has the following composition (w%):
Fe = 92.7, C = 4 Si = 2, P = 0.9 and Mn = 0.4

The furnace consume 900 Kg of coke and 450 Kg of flue for each ton of pig iron.
During the process 99.5% of Fe mass is reduced and the 0.5% is lost in the slag.
The CO/CO2 ratio in the top gas is 2/1.
