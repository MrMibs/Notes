#nmrms 

We REALLY want the molecular ion in our MS. This is possible! We can derivatize our molecule. This means we alter the physicochemical properties which improves compatibility with a method. It can also add "marker/reporter ions", meaning we can react with a specific group and if the thing we react with that group is in our sample, we know we have that group.

An example is GC-EI-MS (the standard LRMS with hard ionization). Here, derivatization can e.g. be
- Silylation: $XH \rightarrow XSiR_3$
- Acetylation: $XH \rightarrow XC=OMe$
- Methylation: $XH \rightarrow XMe$
- Fluoroalkylation: $XH \rightarrow XC=OCF_3$

General idea being:
1. decreased polarity $\rightarrow$ increased volatility which we like in GC as we want to be able to get our molecule in gas phase. 
2. This also suppresses thermal decomposition by increasing thermal stability. 
3. Lastly we can control fragmentation as we know what they are and how they act.
