NOTE: In 2d NMR we have 2 types of peaks cross peaks and diagonal peaks. Diagonal are on the diagonal and cross are the others. Now to the live notes:

We cant discern between 5 and 6
![[Pasted image 20260527132409.png]]
2D NMR lets you plot spectra against themselves to see what couples to what.
![[Pasted image 20260527131327.png]]

Here we have 2 types of peaks, diagonal which say exactly the same as 1d (ppm for a given peak). Cross peaks are interesting as they have different chemical shifts on different axis. This tells you there is a correlation between the atom for one shift and the atom for another. This correlation depends on the spectrum, for this it is a [[COSY sequence]] spectrum. COSY tells us which peaks are scalar coupled.

Say we can guess the peak at the top is nr. 3, it only scalar couples to one, which is nr. 4 (as expected because 2 has no hydrogen). Also 5 and 6 can be discerned as left part couples to nr. 7 and right part to nr. 4.

We can again use 2D nmr to discern between carbon 4, 5, and 6. This can be done using a [[HSQC sequence]] spectrum. It shows the 1 bond couplings between hydrogen and carbon.
![[Pasted image 20260527133438.png]]

Here crosspeaks are interesting as they show the hydrogen corrosponding to carboms. We know the numbers on top from the COSY and can plot them against the HSQC. Convention also states evolution on y axis, acquisition on x-axis.
