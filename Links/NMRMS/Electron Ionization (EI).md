#nmrms
EI is an universal hard ionization method for gasses, it is simple, effective, sensitive, and readily used (thereby library search is available). Sample requirements are
- Gas
- Volatile (entirely evaporable molecule)
- Thermally stable (does not get blown up to crazy fragments by electrons)
- 1000 Da or less (low mass range)
It leads to extensive fragmentation, which is kind of the point. Then you can identify based on the components that make up the molecule you are looking at.

![[Pasted image 20260528140720.png]]

It works by introducing a sample into the ion volume (center area of the ion source block). The filament is then heated to a temperature where it emits electrons by thermionic emission, which produces the electron beam used for ionization (about 2000K or 70eV pr electron = 70V). This is a lot of energy. They fly into the sample and knock off more electrons creating $M^{+\cdot}$ radicals. The leftover electrons are captured by electron trap. This ionized sample is then pushed toward the lenses by the repeller focused through said lenses on its way to the mass analyzer. The result of this is:

![[Pasted image 20260528141950.png]]
You can either
- Ionize sample
- Knock something off
- Rearrange parts together
- Form ion pairs
All this can also be done with anions. The energy of EI is very important as it generates different spectra, almost everyone uses 70 eV
