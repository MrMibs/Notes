#inorganic
Solvent properties:
1) The temperature and pressure range over which it is a liquid.
2) Its dielectric constant.
3) Its donor and acceptor (Lewis acid-base) properties.
4) Its prontonic (Brønsted) acidity or basicity.
5) The nature and extent of autodissociation.

3 types:
## Polar Protic Solvents
The solvents containing ionizable protons are called protic solvents, e.g. H2O, HCl, HF, and H2SO4. They are more or less acidic. Several examples of autodissociation reaction:
2H2O = H3O+ + OH-
2HCl = H2Cl+ + Cl-
2HF = H2F+ + F-

## Polar aprotic solvents
For instance, acetonitrile (CH3CN), acetone (CH3)2CO N,N- dimethylformamide (DMF). They have the features: *aprotic and no auto dissociation equilibria, but they strongly solvates ions

## Nonpolar, or very weakly polar, nondissociated liquids, which do not solvate strongly
For instance, carbon tetrachloride and hydrocarbons are not powerful solvents due to their low polarity, low dielectric constants, and poor donor power. They can be used when a inert solvent is needed.
