FT'ing this would result in the 2 expected peaks. However, if this goes very fast, it switches so rapidly that the waves experience a mix of both signals, resulting in one sharp peak with average frequency. For measurements this means:
$$K_{ex} \ll \Delta \nu \quad \text{2 signals}$$
$$K_{ex} \ll \Delta \nu \quad \text{1 signal}$$
$$K_{ex} = \Delta \nu \quad \text{broad signal / coalessence}$$
where
- $K_{ex}$ is exchange rate \[S]
-  $\Delta \nu$ is difference in resonance frequency
This, along with coalescence temperature at 120, is shown below.

![[Pasted image 20260525144411.png]]

For uncoupled signals of equal intensity at coalescence (quite typical)
$K_{ex}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\Delta\nu$

This is common and happens a lot for rings with Eq and Ax
