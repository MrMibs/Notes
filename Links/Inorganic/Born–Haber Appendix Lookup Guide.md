#inorganic 
## 1. ΔH°atm (Atomization enthalpy)

**Where to find it:**  
Appendix 1: Thermodynamic Properties of Some Selected Inorganic Compounds (A-1)

**What to look for:**

- Formation of gaseous atoms from solid elements  
    Example:  
    Na(s) → Na(g)
- Sometimes listed as:
    - enthalpy of sublimation

---

## 2. ΔH°diss (Bond dissociation energy)

**Where to find it:**  
Appendix 3: Selected Bond Energies (A-16)

**What to look for:**

- Cl–Cl bond energy ≈ 242 kJ/mol

**Use in cycle:**  
(3/2) D(Cl–Cl)

---

## 3. ΔH°1 ion (First ionization energy)

**Where to find it:**  
Appendix 4: Ionization Energies of Selected Metals (A-18)

**What to look for:**  
Na(g) → Na⁺(g) + e⁻ ≈ 496 kJ/mol

---

## 4. ΔH°2 ion and ΔH°3 ion (Higher ionization energies)

**Where to find it:**  
Appendix 4: Ionization Energies of Selected Metals (A-18)

**Typical values:**

- IE2 ≈ 4569 kJ/mol
- IE3 ≈ 6919 kJ/mol

**Important note:**

- These values exist in tables
- They are not chemically realistic for Na in ionic solids
- Used only to demonstrate instability of high oxidation states

---

## 5. ΔH°EA (Electron affinity)

**Where to find it:**  
Appendix 5: Electron Affinities of Selected Nonmetals (A-20)

**For chlorine:**  
Cl(g) + e⁻ → Cl⁻(g) ≈ −349 kJ/mol

---

## 6. U (Lattice energy)

**Where to find it:**  
Appendix 6: Selected Lattice Energies (A-21)

**Given values:**

- NaCl2: −2500 kJ/mol
- NaCl3: −5400 kJ/mol

**Important note:**

- These are already calculated lattice energies
- Not derived using Coulomb’s law in exam problems

---

## Summary of appendix usage

Appendix 1 → atomization enthalpy  
Appendix 3 → bond dissociation energies  
Appendix 4 → ionization energies  
Appendix 5 → electron affinities  
Appendix 6 → lattice energies
