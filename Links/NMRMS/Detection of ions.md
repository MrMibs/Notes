#nmrms
We don't really detect ions, we put them into electron multipliers.
![[Pasted image 20260528165331.png]]
They hit a wall -> chain reaction -> tons of secondary electrons. This can also be done using a microchannel plate.
![[Pasted image 20260528165541.png]]
It works by having a ton of these electron multipliers and based. This allows for measuring the position of impact and since there are so many multipliers you get very good amplifications on a very small plate. You can stack these in chevron and z-stacks which are just multiple of these MCP (microchannel plates) stacked. This however will cost spatial resolution as you don't know where your electrons hit (very easy to see above).

Lastly you measure your current from the many moving electrons which is more detectable than the ion current itself. Thereby higher sensitivity.
