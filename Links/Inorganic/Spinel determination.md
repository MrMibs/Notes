#inorganic 
### **Spinel structure — meaning (what it is)**

A **spinel structure** is a type of ionic crystal structure with general formula:

$$ \mathrm{AB_2O_4} $$

- $$ \mathrm{O^{2-}} $$ ions form a close-packed (fcc) lattice
    
- The cations A and B occupy interstitial sites:
    
    - A²⁺ (or sometimes A¹⁺/A³⁺) in **tetrahedral sites**
        
    - B³⁺ (or B²⁺) in **octahedral sites**
        

So the structure is basically:

> oxygen framework + cations distributed over tetrahedral and octahedral holes

---

## **Two types of spinels**

### 1. **Normal spinel**

$$ (A^{2+})[B_2^{3+}]O_4 $$

- A²⁺ goes into **tetrahedral (Td) sites**
    
- B³⁺ goes into **octahedral (Oh) sites**
    

Example:

- **MgAl₂O₄**
    
    - Mg²⁺ in Td
        
    - Al³⁺ in Oh
        

---

### 2. **Inverse spinel**

$$ (B^{3+})[A^{2+}B^{3+}]O_4 $$

- B³⁺ splits:
    
    - half in Td
        
    - half in Oh
        
- A²⁺ goes to Oh sites
    

Example:

- **Fe₃O₄ (magnetite)**
    
    - Fe²⁺ in Oh
        
    - Fe³⁺ split between Td + Oh
        

---

## **How to determine spinel type (exam method)**

### Step 1: Write oxidation states

Make sure you know A and B charges.

---

### Step 2: Decide cation site preference

### A) Charge density / ionic potential

- higher charge + smaller radius → prefers **octahedral (Oh)**
    

So:

- M³⁺ → usually Oh
    
- M²⁺ → often Td unless special case
    

---

### B) Crystal field stabilization energy (CFSE)

Compare stability in Td vs Oh:

- If CFSE is larger in octahedral → prefers Oh
    
- If tetrahedral gives better stability → goes Td
    

Key idea:

> d⁸, d⁶ (low spin), d³ → often favor octahedral

---

### C) Empirical rule

|Cation|Preferred site|
|---|---|
|A²⁺ (Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺)|usually Td|
|Fe³⁺, Cr³⁺, Al³⁺|usually Oh|
|Fe²⁺|usually Oh (inverse tendency)|
|Zn²⁺|strongly Td|

---

## **Step 3: Check if swapping lowers energy**

Ask:

> Does putting A²⁺ in octahedral sites reduce energy (CFSE or size effects)?

- YES → inverse spinel
    
- NO → normal spinel
    

---

## **Step 4: Final assignment pattern**

### Normal spinel:

- A²⁺ → Td
    
- B³⁺ → Oh
    

### Inverse spinel:

- B³⁺ → split Td + Oh
    
- A²⁺ → Oh
    

---

## **Quick exam shortcut**

- Main group ions → usually **normal spinel**
    
- Transition metals (variable oxidation states) → often **inverse spinel**
    

---

## **Example: Fe₃O₄**

- Fe²⁺ + 2 Fe³⁺
    

Inverse spinel:

- Fe³⁺: Td + Oh
    
- Fe²⁺: Oh
    

Reason: Fe²⁺ strongly prefers octahedral due to CFSE + size effects

---

## **One-line summary**

> Spinel structure = fcc $$ \mathrm{O^{2-}} $$ lattice with cations in tetrahedral and octahedral sites; normal or inverse depends on ionic size, charge, and CFSE-driven site preference.
