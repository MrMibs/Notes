### Step 1: Prepairing precursors
**Blue sulfate solution**: 
2.78g of iron sulfate heptahydrate
5ml DI water
2M solution

**Brown chloride solution**:
5.40g ferric chloride heptahydrite
20 ml DI water
1M solution

Orbital shaker at 120rpm for 20 min to form homogenous solutions. Brown chloride solution stirred at 200RPM. Add blue sulfate solution and stir for another 5.

### Step 2: Introducing the acidic solution
Always add acid to the water, not the other way around

Add the metallic stirring rod and 16ml of DI-water. Stir at 300RPM. Slowly add 8.33mL of HCl to attain 25mL of 4M solution. 

Add to the solution above with pipette and stir for 5 minutes. Color turns yellow.

### Step 3: Introducing the ammonium hydroxide
225.67 mL of DI water for the solution base and add stirring rod. Stir at 300 RPM. Add 24.33 mL of ammonium hydroxide and allow to stir thoroughly achieving a 25 mL of a 0.7M solution.

Gently add this to the solution so far and it turns black. Set to 600 RPM and stir for 30 min.

### Step 4: Cleaning the nanoparticles
Place magnet beneath beaker to let particles form at the bottom. This also checks that it works and you have made them. Carefully dispose of excess liquid using pipette:
![[Pasted image 20260612120809.png]]
Replace stirring rod and set speed to 300 RPM. Add 50 to 100 ml of DI water to rinse, don't let it splash. Stir for 5 minutes. Remove excess liquid. Do this 3 more times.

### Step 5: Redispersion
Add 128 ml of DI-water and 72 mL of TMAH resulting in1M TMAH solution with magnetite nanoparticles. This yields a hydrophobic and magnetic solution.

###  (A) Preparation of Magnetite nanoparticles and Magnetite-Activated Carbon (MAC) Nanocomposite

- Take two 50-mL beakers. In one beaker you will add 4.0 mL of 1M FeCl3 solution and 1.0 mL of 2M FeCl2. In the second beaker you will add 0.1 g of activated carbon, 4.0 mL of 1M FeCl3 solution and 1.0 mL of 2M FeCl2 solution.
- Now the content of the two beakers will follow the same procedure; mix the content of the beakers with a glass rod or a plastic spoon for 2-3 minutes. 
- Add 25 mL of 1.4 M NH3 solution dropwise to the content of the each beaker over a period of about 5 minutes.  Throughout the addition, continuously and vigorously mix the content in the beaker with the glass rod.
- Place the beaker on a magnet to allow the MAC nanocomposite particles to settle.
- Decant and discard the clear liquid without losing a substantial quantity of solid. This works best if you keep a magnet under the container.
- Rinse with 25 mL deionized water, stir, and decant the rinse with the magnet under the container as before.
- Repeat the step above for two times.  

### (B) Adsorption tests

Magnetite nanoparticle prepared in absence and in the presence of active carbon  (AC) will be tested for their ability to remove a dye from water. Make the two powders in fine particles with mortar and pestle.

- A solution of Rhodamine B (3 ppm) in 250 mL of distilled water is made and light absorbance (A0) is measured at 553 nm.

- The Rhodamine B solution is used to prepare two suspensions:

- Suspension 1: Magnetite with no AC (800 ppm) in 100 mL of Rhodamine B solution in a 250 mL flask;

- Suspension 2: Magnetite with AC (800 ppm) in 100 mL of Rhodamine B solution in a 250 mL flask;

  

- Alumina foil is used to cover the flask, avoiding any interaction with the light, as impurities can act as light sensitizer and promote Rhodamine B photodegradation. Suspensions are stirred and 2-3 mL aliquots of the suspensions are collected in 15’-30’-45’-1h, filtered through syringe over 0.45 μm (PTFE) filters, and light absorbance is measured at 550 nm.

- The abatement of Rhodamine B  in solution (Abatment%) is calculated through this formula:

Abatement% = $(C0-C) \cdot 100/C0$

where C0 is the ce starting Rhodamine B concentration and C is the value for the specific sample, after a certain abatement time.

- Plot Abatement% as a function of the time and discuss the graph.
