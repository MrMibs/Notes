## Definition
Base rate neglect occurs when ignoring the underlying probability (base rate) of an event and focusing too much on specific evidence.

## Core idea
Prior probabilities matter, even if a signal seems strong.

## Example
- A medical test is 99% accurate
- Disease is very rare (0.1%)
→ Most positive results are still false positives

## Why it is a problem
- Leads to overly confident but wrong conclusions
- Misinterprets conditional probabilities

## Key intuition
Rare events stay rare, even if tests look accurate.

## When it matters
- Medical diagnosis
- Risk prediction
- Classification problems with imbalanced data
