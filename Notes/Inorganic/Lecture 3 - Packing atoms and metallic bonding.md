Bonds normally look like this:
![[Pasted image 20260610101742.png]]
With electrons being shared to some degree. In metallic bonds however, nothing has high electronegativity and therefore electrons are more or less free to move around (electron sea)
![[Pasted image 20260610101855.png]]
This theory can give an explanation of the characteristic properties of metals.
1) The orbitals of neighbouring atoms overlap and interact with each other.
2) The interaction forms continuous energy bands.
3) The electrons in these bands are completely delocalized
As stated above these continous sharings of electrons are called **bands**. 
![[Pasted image 20260610102010.png]]
This looks like this:
![[Pasted image 20260610102504.png]]
Where we fill $\sigma 2s$ molecular orbitals continuously. These bands can, as molecular orbitals do, have varying energy levels.
![[Pasted image 20260610103011.png]]
Which leads to varying properties:
![[Pasted image 20260610103033.png]]
Based on the energy difference between a metal in bonding and antibonding orbitals, we see very different properties. If the difference is not that big, we get a conductor as electrons can enter this state to achieve a temporary state of higher energy.
