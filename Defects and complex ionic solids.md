Defects exist as either Schottky defects with missing ions, Frenkel Defects with ions in interstitial sites. 

Further, extrinsic defects i.e. defects in a crystal lattice caused by the **presence of impurity atoms (foreign species)** rather than missing or misplaced atoms of the host material exist such as non stoichiometric compounds. These are solids where the **ideal integer ratio of elements is not strictly obeyed**, often because of defects and here the crystal stays neutral by adjusting oxidation states or defect concentrations. 

Or doping, which is the deliberate introduction of **foreign atoms into a crystal** to change its properties. This creates extrinsic charge carriers & strongly changes conductivity (especially in semiconductors)

The lattice energy (enthalpy) is the energy released by the formation of 1 mol of an ionic solid from its constituent gaseous ions.

Inorganic solids: a 3D array of ions. Energy of this array of ions includes:
1) Coulombic attractive and repulsive energies;
2) Additional repulsive energy resulting from repulsion between the overlapping outer electron density of adjacent ions;
3) van der Waals energy and zero point vibrational energy.

This lattice energy can be calculated as:
$$E=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac{e^2}{r_0}=\frac{A\cdot e^2}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0  \cdot r_0}$$
where:
- E is lattice energy
- $\varepsilon_0$ is vacuum dialectric constant ($8.854\cdot10^{-12} \, C^2 \, m^{-1} \, J^{-1}$)
- $e$ is electron charge in columbs ($-1.602 \cdot 10^{-19} \, C$)
- $r_0$ is equilibrium interionic distance i.e. distance between the centers of two ions in the crystal at their most stable configuration
- $A$ is the Madelung constant to account for many contributions from many ions
